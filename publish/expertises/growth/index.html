Adoption & Growth Engineering

Turn scattered capability and commercial context into systems your team can measure, reuse and improve.

AI access is not the same thing as transferable capability. Marketing activity is not the same thing as a reusable growth system. Make roles, context, evidence, tests and next actions explicit before scaling what you do.

Two tracks. One operating principle: make the work observable before scaling it.

Two operating problems

Choose the bottleneck, not a forced bundle.

The existing Growth category remains a routing layer. Training is for capability transfer. Marketing Engineering is for commercial context, measurement and reusable decision logic.

Track A

AI Training & Adoption Engineering — Governed Capability Transfer

For teams that need governed capability transfer, not attendance as a proxy for adoption.

  • Role / task / competency matrix
  • Scenario-based exercises tied to real work
  • Assessment rubrics and escalation rules
  • Data-classification and human/AI boundaries
  • Transfer-evidence protocol and handover kit
Which tasks should change, what competence is required, what remains human-owned, and what evidence is enough to accept transfer?

Format: framing, a pilot cohort of at most 12, then a 3–6 month programme when expansion is justified.

Track B

Marketing Engineering & Context Systems

For teams whose buyer knowledge and campaign logic are scattered across people, slides, CRM fields, prompts and disconnected analytics.

  • Versioned avatars and buyer questions
  • Offer and positioning architecture
  • Source and evidence register
  • Message / hook hypotheses
  • Metric contracts, experiment definitions and decision logs
Which buyer hypothesis are we testing, what evidence supports it, which signal changes the next action, and how will that learning be reused?

Format: 3–6 month marketing operating-system and automation engagement.

Delivery

Frame one decision. Build the minimum operating layer. Test it on bounded work.

The engagement is deliberately staged so observations and negative results remain visible instead of being rewritten to fit the preferred narrative.

01 · Frame

Define the decision

Name the accountable owner, target users, current workflow, constraints and acceptance criteria.

02 · Baseline

Inspect the current system

Inventory sources, prompts, training assets, CRM/analytics fields, handoffs and known failure modes.

03 · Design

Build only what the decision needs

Create the selected track's minimum set of context, capability, measurement and governance objects.

04 · Pilot

Use bounded work

Run a small cohort or a bounded buyer / offer / message / workflow slice.

05 · Review

Preserve the observations

Measure predeclared signals and keep positive, negative and null outcomes.

06 · Transfer

Leave the system operable

Handover rules, reusable assets, evidence register, unresolved risks and next-test backlog.

Acceptance outcomes

What should become observable or reusable after the work.

These are bounded deliverable outcomes. They are not guarantees of revenue growth, productivity uplift, compliance or a universal adoption rate.

Outcome 1

Observable capability

Managers can point to target tasks, rubrics, escalation rules and transfer evidence instead of treating participation as proof of adoption.

Outcome 2

Reusable commercial context

Avatars, offers, evidence, messages, prompts and metrics are versioned rather than rebuilt for every campaign or operator.

Outcome 3

A decision loop that survives handover

The team can distinguish what was observed, what remains a hypothesis, what decision was made, who owns it and what should be tested next.

Capacity & risk reversal

No artificial countdown. No guarantee beyond controllable delivery.

The only current published capacity constraint in this category is the Training pilot of at most 12 participants.

Scarcity

Operational capacity, not manufactured urgency.

Training pilots are capped at 12 participants. Other engagement availability is confirmed during qualification; no invented “last slot” scarcity is used.

Guarantee proposal

Acceptance criteria before external outcome claims.

Each deliverable should have written acceptance criteria before build. Any remediation promise must be contractually approved before publication. Revenue, productivity, compliance and adoption remain outside the guarantee unless separately and validly evidenced.

FAQ

What still blocks a decision?

Use the Growth page to route the operating problem before selecting tools or delivery components.

Is this a marketing agency?

No. Marketing Engineering builds the context, measurement and decision system around marketing execution. It can coexist with internal marketers, agencies and media specialists.

Is this an AI training course?

Not by default. The Training track starts from target workplace tasks, competence, human/AI boundaries and transfer evidence. A course can be one delivery component.

Do we need both tracks?

No. Use Training when the bottleneck is capability transfer. Use Marketing Engineering when the bottleneck is commercial context and measurement. Combine them only when both belong to one operating problem.

Can you guarantee revenue growth?

No. The engagement can improve the quality and traceability of decisions and tests; uncontrolled market outcomes cannot be guaranteed truthfully.

Can you guarantee a specific AI-adoption rate?

No. The work defines target tasks and acceptance evidence so adoption can be evaluated in context.

Do you replace our CRM, LMS or analytics stack?

Not by default. The decision layer is structured around systems already in use unless replacement is independently justified.

Can the system automate decisions?

Bounded steps can be automated when authority, acceptance and escalation are explicit. Consequential decisions retain named ownership unless governance explicitly says otherwise.

What happens to failed experiments?

They remain in the evidence register with the same provenance discipline as positive observations.
