Make one pharmaceutical AI decision reconstructable before it becomes an inspection problem.

For Quality, Pharmaceutical Operations and technical co-owners introducing AI into a bounded GxP workflow: preserve source provenance, explicit decision rights and accountable human judgement from evidence to release decision.

Triggers

Start from a dated decision event, not a generic “AI transformation”.

The sprint is justified when a named decision is becoming hard to defend because evidence, controls or ownership are fragmented.

Consequences

The risk is not “using AI”. It is losing the ability to show why a material decision was acceptable.

Separate pharmaceutical authority from technical automation. Design the evidence path so a reviewer can see the source, transformation, control, system state, exception and human judgement that produced the result.

Fragmented evidence

eQMS, LIMS, batch records, spreadsheets and CDMO portals disagree on what is authoritative.

Deliverable: a product-centred evidence map naming the source of truth, owner, version and known gap for each material decision.

Plausible AI output

The model sounds convincing, but the system cannot show which input boundary, permission or tool state produced it.

NCSC’s 2026 agentic-AI guidance places pre-deployment threat modelling around the prompt and the networks/services reachable by the configured agent.[3]

Late reconstruction

Evidence is assembled after the question is asked.

NCSC separately identifies agent traces/transcripts and wider sandbox logs as telemetry for active oversight and later investigation.[4]

Unbounded privilege

A workflow can call more systems than the task actually needs.

The same guidance describes four network-isolation levels, from unrestricted access to no external network access with local model hosting.[5]

Frustrations & fears

  • Inspection or release decision arrives before the evidence chain is reconstructable.
  • Quality has to reconcile conflicting source versions after the fact.
  • “Closed” deviations or CAPAs cannot be cleanly tied to the evidence used in the next decision.
  • An AI-assisted step introduces opaque inputs, permissions, versions or transformations.
  • Accountability remains with the pharmaceutical organisation while execution is split across technology teams, sites or partners.

Boundary: these are page-trigger and buyer-model hypotheses grounded in the current programme. Frequency and severity require client data.

Wants & aspirations

  • A validation approach proportionate to product/process risk, with traceable requirements and explicit acceptance criteria.[2]
  • An authoritative-source map that survives hand-offs between Quality, Operations, Data/AI and external partners.
  • AI/data controls that preserve human pharmaceutical judgement rather than silently transfer it to a model.
  • Exceptions and residual uncertainty visible early enough to make a bounded decision.
  • A reusable decision dossier that makes review about exceptions and judgement, not repeated context reconstruction.

Qualification

Decide fit before you share a single regulated record.

A twenty-minute “no” is cheaper than a quarter spent forcing the wrong engagement.

Good fit

  • A real production, audit, transfer, launch or replacement decision is in scope.
  • A named sponsor can arbitrate scope and acceptance.
  • Required systems or documents can be accessed under NDA.
  • AI touches a GxP workflow and the validation / assurance boundary is still open.
  • Quality can review deliverables during the sprint, not only at the end.

No fit

  • You want a trend report or generic AI workshop.
  • You need a statutory signature or batch-release decision from the consultant.
  • No evidence access or internal owner can be named.
  • You expect a guaranteed inspection outcome.
  • You want SOP templates without examining the decision process that they control.

Pharmaceutical risk stays pharmaceutical.

AI does not become an authority because it is accurate on a benchmark. Its function is bounded to collection, extraction, comparison, detection or synthesis; the human decision right remains explicit.

Risk management stays iterative.

NIST AI RMF organises AI risk work into Govern, Map, Measure and Manage, and states that the functions are not an ordered checklist.[7]

Where EU AI Act classification is relevant, the European Commission’s overview distinguishes four risk levels; logging and human-oversight requirements are linked to high-risk systems rather than every AI use.[6]

Scope

One decision boundary. Explicit exclusions.

Engineering of evidence, structured analysis and technical support to the accountable pharmaceutical organisation — never delegated statutory authority.

In scope

  • One product, site, MAH–CDMO relation, transfer programme, inspection perimeter or critical quality process.
  • Evidence lineage and source-of-truth designation.
  • AI/data permissions, logs, evaluation and rollback requirements.
  • Decision dossier, exception handling and transfer controls.

Out of scope

  • Batch certification or release.
  • Replacing the QP, Pharmacien Responsable, QA, QC, CMC, Regulatory Affairs or Pharmacovigilance.
  • Legal advice or guaranteed regulatory approval.
  • Guaranteed inspection outcome or absence of future deviation.
  • Autonomous pharmaceutical decision-making.
Process

Six weeks, with a stop point before implementation.

If the decision boundary, evidence access or governance cannot support a defensible architecture by the end of week 2, stop rather than force the build.

Before · written qualification

Product/workflow, trigger, decision, accountable roles, evidence systems, confidentiality constraints and intended AI use.

Week 1 · decision and risk perimeter

Included/excluded systems, access conditions, data classification, owners, deadlines, assumptions and acceptance criteria.

Week 2 · evidence and responsibility map

Requirement → control → evidence → decision owner. Viability gate before implementation.

Week 3 · AI/data architecture and permissions

Authorised sources, tool and network reach, retention, logs, validation points and escalation paths.

Week 4 · evaluation and decision dossier

Test scenarios, failure classes, acceptance thresholds, human review, exception handling and residual-risk language.

Week 5 · controlled implementation / prototype

Instrument the selected evidence workflow and generate proof from execution, not from a retrospective note.

Week 6 · executive review + transfer

Separate confirmed fact, observation, hypothesis, unresolved evidence gap, accepted residual risk and management decision.

Commitments

Guarantee the work product and the stop condition — never an authority’s decision.

By the end of week 2, the minimum preparation package is either reviewable or the implementation phase does not proceed.

Guaranteed preparation gate

Written decision perimeter, evidence/source map, material-gap register, responsibility matrix and viability recommendation — conditional on the agreed inputs being provided.

Explicit non-guarantees

Questions

Resolve the objections before confidential evidence moves.

Cost, governance, validation burden, black-box concerns, CDMO boundaries, confidentiality and statutory accountability should be explicit at qualification.

Why not give this to our Quality or Data team?

The programme is useful only where the seam is the problem: Quality owns pharmaceutical acceptability; engineering owns system behaviour. The engagement creates a shared decision and evidence contract, then hands it back to both teams.

Will governance slow innovation?

Governance is not treated as a late approval layer. The work defines authorised source, action, permission and evidence boundaries before implementation, so the team knows what can move quickly and what must stop for review.

Can ChatGPT or another LLM be the system?

No general-purpose model is assumed to be a qualified production system. The model is one component inside an architecture with source boundaries, permissions, logging, evaluation and accountable review.

How do you defend a black-box model?

You defend the bounded use: authorised inputs, validated workflow, observed failure modes, protected logs, reviewer criteria and the human decision that remains accountable.

Can the service work with our existing QMS and data platform?

Yes, if the existing systems can expose the required evidence under controlled access. The default is integration with designated sources of truth, not wholesale replacement.

How are confidential data protected?

The engagement uses explicit source scope, data minimisation, least privilege and controlled disclosure. Technical-control research is used only within its study boundary; it is not a universal privacy guarantee.
