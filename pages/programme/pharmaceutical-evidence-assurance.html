Decision boundary 1 workflow

One product, one material decision path, or one operational event. Six weeks. Viability gate at the end of week 2.

Technical consulting and assurance only. The programme never certifies or releases a batch and never replaces the Qualified Person, Pharmacien Responsable or another statutory pharmaceutical authority.

Triggers

Start from a dated decision event, not a generic “AI transformation”.

The sprint is justified when a named decision is becoming hard to defend because evidence, controls or ownership are fragmented.

01

Inspection announced

The date is fixed; the organisation cannot yet show which evidence chain supports the decision under review.

02

Batch review repeatedly delayed

Reviewers keep reconstructing missing context instead of evaluating bounded exceptions.

03

Recurring deviation / CAPA pattern

Closure status exists, but the connection from intervention to expected observable effect is weak.

04

Technology transfer

The process moves between sites or partners and prior decision rationale is not portable.

05

Launch readiness

The first commercial or critical milestone approaches while the evidence path still depends on tacit knowledge.

06

MAH–CDMO boundary

Execution is externalised while pharmaceutical accountability remains internal.

07

OOS / OOT recurrence

Similar events are treated as isolated cases because classification and context differ across teams.

08

AI-assisted workflow promotion

Sources, model versions, permissions, evaluations or human approval points are not yet reconstructable.

Consequences

The risk is not “using AI”. It is losing the ability to show why a material decision was acceptable.

Separate pharmaceutical authority from technical automation. Design the evidence path so a reviewer can see the source, transformation, control, model/tool state, exception and human judgement that produced the result.

Fragmented evidence

eQMS, LIMS, batch records, spreadsheets and CDMO portals disagree on what is authoritative.

Deliverable: a product-centred evidence map naming the source of truth, owner, version and known gap for each material decision.

Plausible AI output

The model sounds convincing, but the system cannot show which input boundary, permission or tool state produced it.

For whom

A decision unit, not a fictional single buyer.

The primary avatar is the Quality / CSV authority who must defend the decision. The technical co-owner makes the evidence executable; operations owns the workflow; adoption roles make the control durable.

Primary authority

Quality Director / CSV lead / QP-facing quality role

Needs a bounded risk argument, named source owners, review criteria, residual-risk language and a defensible hand-off to statutory authority.

Technical co-owner

CTO / Enterprise Architecture / Data Platform

Needs implementable boundaries: services, IAM, logs, data contracts, evaluation gates, observability and rollback.

Workflow owner

Pharmaceutical Operations / CMC / site quality

Needs fewer context-reconstruction loops without transferring the final pharmaceutical decision to a model.

Qualification

Decide fit before you share a single regulated record.

A twenty-minute “no” is cheaper than a quarter spent forcing the wrong engagement.

Good fit

  • A real production, audit, transfer, launch or replacement decision is in scope.
  • A named sponsor can arbitrate scope and acceptance.
  • Required systems or documents can be accessed under NDA.
  • AI touches a GxP workflow and the validation / assurance boundary is still open.
  • Quality can review deliverables during the sprint, not only at the end.

No fit

  • You want a trend report or generic AI workshop.
  • You need a statutory signature or batch-release decision from the consultant.
  • No evidence access or internal owner can be named.
  • You expect a guaranteed inspection outcome.
  • You want SOP templates without examining the decision process that they control.

Scope

One decision boundary. Explicit exclusions.

Engineering of evidence, structured analysis and technical support to the accountable pharmaceutical organisation — never delegated statutory authority.

In scope

  • One product, site, MAH–CDMO relation, transfer programme, inspection perimeter or critical quality process.
  • Evidence lineage and source-of-truth designation.
  • AI/data permissions, logs, evaluation and rollback requirements.
  • Decision dossier, exception handling and transfer controls.

Out of scope

  • Batch certification or release.
  • Replacing the QP, Pharmacien Responsable, QA, QC, CMC, Regulatory Affairs or Pharmacovigilance.
  • Legal advice or guaranteed regulatory approval.
  • Guaranteed inspection outcome or absence of future deviation.
  • Autonomous pharmaceutical decision-making.
Process

Six weeks, with a stop point before implementation.

If the decision boundary, evidence access or governance cannot support a defensible architecture by the end of week 2, stop rather than force the build.

Before · written qualification

Product/workflow, trigger, decision, accountable roles, evidence systems, confidentiality constraints and intended AI use.

Week 1 · decision and risk perimeter

Included/excluded systems, access conditions, data classification, owners, deadlines, assumptions and acceptance criteria.

Week 2 · evidence and responsibility map

Requirement → control → evidence → decision owner. Viability gate before implementation.

Week 3 · AI/data architecture and permissions

Authorised sources, tool and network reach, retention, logs, validation points and escalation paths.

Week 4 · evaluation and decision dossier

Test scenarios, failure classes, acceptance thresholds, human review, exception handling and residual-risk language.

Week 5 · controlled implementation / prototype

Instrument the selected evidence workflow and generate proof from execution, not from a retrospective note.

Week 6 · executive review + transfer

Separate confirmed fact, observation, hypothesis, unresolved evidence gap, accepted residual risk and management decision.

Modules

Use the minimum control mechanism that closes the decision gap.

Modules can be removed from scope when the client already controls them. The architecture is not a pretext to sell every technical capability.

Evidence integrity & source lineage

Authoritative systems, version boundaries, inaccessible/contradictory evidence, transformations and reviewer trace.

Product-centred risk governance

Connect gaps to patient, product, batch, submission, supply and inspection consequences without inventing probabilities.

Intersection

The differentiator is the seam between pharmaceutical judgement and information architecture.

Regulated operations define what must remain defensible. Data and AI engineering define how the evidence, permissions, versions and tests can remain inspectable. Neither discipline should silently substitute for the other.

Regulated pharmaceutical operations

Community pharmacy 2015–2023 plus hospital radiopharmacy quality-control experience in 2018–2019, represented as bounded public professional evidence.

AI & data engineering

Python, SQL, service architecture, evidence stores, IAM/RBAC, evaluation harnesses and observable workflows.

Assurance & verifiability

Risk-to-requirement mapping, explicit source boundaries, protected logs, deterministic checks and reviewable promotion gates.

Commitments

Guarantee the work product and the stop condition — never an authority’s decision.

By the end of week 2, the minimum preparation package is either reviewable or the implementation phase does not proceed.

Guaranteed preparation gate

Written decision perimeter, evidence/source map, material-gap register, responsibility matrix and viability recommendation — conditional on the agreed inputs being provided.

Explicit non-guarantees

No regulatory approval, inspection result, batch certification/release, future-deviation absence, or decision controlled by a regulator, CDMO, supplier or client statutory role.

Human approval is necessary in many workflows, but “ask a human” is not by itself a complete control: in one controlled 2026 study, 133 of 148 executed overreach actions in the POLICY condition followed affirmative human approval.

Questions

Resolve the objections before confidential evidence moves.

Cost, governance, validation burden, black-box concerns, CDMO boundaries, confidentiality and statutory accountability should be explicit at qualification.

Why not give this to our Quality or Data team?

The programme is useful only where the seam is the problem: Quality owns pharmaceutical acceptability; engineering owns system behaviour. The engagement creates a shared decision and evidence contract, then hands it back to both teams.

Will governance slow innovation?

Governance is not treated as a late approval layer. The work defines the authorised source, action, permission and evidence boundaries before implementation, so the team knows what can move quickly and what must stop for review.

Can ChatGPT or another LLM be the system?

No general-purpose model is assumed to be a qualified production system. The model is one component inside an architecture with source boundaries, permissions, logging, evaluation and accountable review.

How do you defend a black-box model?

You do not convert a model into a statutory authority. You defend the bounded use: authorised inputs, validated workflow, observed failure modes, protected logs, reviewer criteria and the human decision that remains accountable.

Can the service work with our existing QMS and data platform?

Yes, if the existing systems can expose the required evidence under controlled access. The default is integration with designated sources of truth, not wholesale replacement.

How are confidential data protected?

The engagement uses explicit source scope, data minimisation, least privilege and controlled disclosure. For the bounded research example on schema-aware minimisation, see evidence [9] in Deliverables; it remains a study-specific metric, not a universal privacy guarantee.

Engagement

Start with the decision — not a sales call.

Send a short brief containing the product or workflow, decision to be made, trigger, deadline, accountable pharmaceutical role, systems containing the evidence, intended AI use, and the cost of leaving the decision unresolved. Do not send batch records, deviation details or patient data at qualification.
