Claire Dumas

AGENTIC AI
Before

Agents pass demos, but ownership, permissions, memory, evaluation and production behaviour are not yet controllable. Threat modelling now has to include both the prompt and the networks or services an agent can reach.[1]

EU-SG-003 · NCSC · 20 Aug 2026NCSC names the prompt and reachable networks/services as threat-modelling inputs and says threat modelling should occur before deployment.Normative guidance; it does not quantify incident reduction.Primary source
The same guidance groups seven operational considerations around threat modelling, oversight, sandboxing, observability, attribution and shutdown.[1]
EU-SG-002Seven operational considerations in NCSC interim guidance.Guidance structure, not a measured production effect.
 AgentDojo already evaluates tool-using agents across 97 realistic tasks and 629 security test cases.[2]
EU-AGENTIC-AGENTDOJO-629-2024 · NeurIPS 202497 realistic tasks; 629 security test cases.Benchmark scope; not an enterprise incident-rate estimate.Primary source

After

A bounded, observable runtime with an explicit owner, explicit authority, governed context and reusable execution evidence. NCSC explicitly calls out agent traces/transcripts plus wider sandbox logs for oversight and investigation,[1]

EU-SG-004Agent traces/transcripts plus wider sandbox logs; protect logs and make them immutable where possible.Operational guidance, not a quantified audit-success effect.
describes four levels of network isolation,[1]
EU-SG-007Four network-isolation levels from unrestricted access to fully local/no external network.Maturity structure, not a security-effect curve.
 and separately calls for safeguards, real-time oversight and clear response plans from the outset.[3]
EU-SG-001 · NCSC · 4 Aug 2026Strong safeguards, real-time oversight and clear response plans; post-incident detection alone is insufficient.Government cybersecurity statement.Primary source

Decision principle. “Human approval” is not by itself a control architecture. In one controlled user study, 89.9% of executed overreach under a user-authored POLICY condition followed affirmative human approval.[4]
EU-AISEC-RUN-34 · controlled user study113 participants; 18-action simulated day; 148 POLICY overreach actions executed, 133 after affirmative approval.Descriptive laboratory result; not a production incident estimate.Open paper
 Minimise what reaches a tool as well: ToolMinimize reports a 79.0% reduction in its study-specific privacy-cost metric on 25 unannotated MCP schemas.[5]
EU-AISEC-RUN-2279.0% reduction in the paper's study-specific privacy-cost metric.25 MCP schemas; not a universal privacy-risk reduction.Open paper
Externalize authority for consequential actions. In the Bounded Agents compromised-model evaluation, external authorization reduced observed exfiltration to 0% across each reported AgentDojo exfiltration category,[6]
EU-CANON-BOUNDED-AGENTS-EXFIL-2026AgentDojo exfiltration rows were 75–100% without defense and 0% with APC.Protocol-bounded controlled evaluation; documented utility trade-offs; no universal prevention claim.Open paper
 and the paper also evaluates 544 InjecAgent data-stealing cases.[6]
EU-AGENTIC-BOUNDED-INJEC-544-2026544 evaluated InjecAgent data-stealing cases.Evaluation population only.
Regulatory context is separate from security-effect evidence. The European Commission’s current AI Act overview distinguishes four risk levels.[7]
EU-SG-005 · European CommissionFour risk levels in the Commission overview.Classification and obligations depend on the actual system; this page is not legal advice.Primary source

Authorises the spend

Needs economic scope, accountability and a defensible delivery boundary.

Advocate

The current page uses an eight-screen qualification flow. This static reconstruction keeps its decision logic visible without writing data anywhere.

Open the live qualification flow
01 · Objective — What business or technical outcome must change?
02 · Timeline — Now, month, quarter or later?
03 · Urgency — Live deadline, decision window, planned or exploring?
04 · Buying role — Decision maker, sponsor, recommender or exploring?
05 · Budget readiness — Confirmed, approval, estimate or below?
06 · Scope clarity — Brief ready, outcome known, problem known or idea?
07 · Delivery shape — Duration, weekly commitment, company size and impact scope.
08 · Contact — Submit only through the live consented route.
