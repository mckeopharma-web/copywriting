## 1. Emotional core

### SYNTHESIS — Primary brand emotion

**Controlled competence.**

The homepage should make complex technical work feel **inspectable, bounded, governable and transferable**. The intended emotional movement is not “uncertainty → excitement”; it is:

> **uncertainty → explicit structure → evidence → controlled confidence**

### One Emotion Sentence

**Rendre la complexité inspectable, bornée et reprise en main.**

This is the anchor sentence for briefs, visual reviews, copy generation and design decisions.

### OBSERVED source anchors

The homepage repeatedly frames value through production readiness, auditability, reversibility, explicit limits, permissions, evaluations, provenance, traceability and human ownership. Its hero promise is that AI and data systems can be put into production and then understood, audited and defended. The live page also emphasizes written scope before billing, complete/reworked deliverables and reversibility at exit.

## 2. Brand emotional axes

### SYNTHESIS

The brand should consistently sit toward the right side of these axes:

| Avoid | Brand position |
|---|---|
| Hype | Proof |
| Opaque | Inspectable |
| Dependency | Transfer / takeover |
| Demo | Production |
| Promise | Explicit boundary |
| Decorative complexity | Operational clarity |
| Authority by assertion | Authority by traceability |
| Automation-first | Decision-ownership-first |

The brand is therefore **not anti-innovation**. It is anti-ambiguity about how innovation operates, fails, escalates, gets reviewed and gets transferred.

## 3. Ideal client avatar — inner world

The homepage exposes three primary decision situations. The interpretation below preserves those roles but describes the emotional state behind the task.

### CTO / Head of AI Platform

**OBSERVED situation:** convincing agents or demos without a documented/governed runtime.

**SYNTHESIS inner state:**
- tension: “I can show capability, but I cannot yet defend operational ownership.”
- feared outcome: hidden permissions, uncontrolled runtime behavior, brittle handover, late security discovery.
- desired feeling: **control** — architecture, permissions, observability, evaluation and promotion criteria are explicit.
- proof trigger: an implementation artifact or runtime boundary that another engineer can inspect and take over.

### Quality / CSV / regulated operations

**OBSERVED situation:** evidence and documentation reconstructed after the fact; an inspection or regulated decision is approaching.

**SYNTHESIS inner state:**
- tension: “The work may be correct, but the decision chain is not reliably reconstructable.”
- feared outcome: evidence gaps, unclear responsibility, retrospective justification.
- desired feeling: **defensibility** — requirements, data, controls, exceptions and human decisions remain linked.
- proof trigger: an evidence chain with named boundaries, source provenance and explicit human responsibility.

### AI transformation / training / adoption

**OBSERVED situation:** AI use remains individual or cannot be evidenced across teams.

**SYNTHESIS inner state:**
- tension: “Adoption exists, but organizational capability is not observable.”
- feared outcome: superficial training, hidden risk, expertise that disappears with individuals.
- desired feeling: **transfer** — skills become observable and teams know when to verify or escalate.
- proof trigger: role-specific tasks, assessment criteria, rules of escalation and reusable context.

### Shared emotional job

Across the three situations, the shared job is:

**Replace invisible operational debt with an explicit system that can be inspected, challenged and owned by the client.**

## 4. Founder energy

### OBSERVED source anchors

The homepage states that the same person scopes the mission, executes it and transfers it; it also connects the proof posture to prior work in regulated health operations. The consultant identity is presented as AI systems engineer + data engineer, with public implementation provenance where available and explicit NDA boundaries where not.

### SYNTHESIS — Three-part founder archetype

**Builder** — converts ambiguous needs into working systems and artifacts.

**Auditor** — treats evidence, provenance, controls and boundaries as first-class engineering objects.

**Translator** — links AI/data architecture, regulated operations and decision ownership without pretending the disciplines are identical.

The founder energy should feel **close to the work, technically literate and accountable**, not “visionary at a distance.”

## 5. Voice system

### Voice character

**Precise · sober · bounded · verifiable · operational.**

The brand should sound like an engineer explaining a system before someone signs off on it.

### Sentence behavior

- Prefer concrete subject → mechanism → boundary → outcome.
- State what changes operationally before abstract value.
- Use named artifacts and decision points rather than generic “transformation”.
- Put limits next to claims, not in footnotes after persuasion.
- Distinguish observed evidence, derived interpretation and planned outcomes.
- Use active verbs that imply ownership and inspection.

### Preferred verbs

`cadrer` · `tracer` · `relier` · `vérifier` · `évaluer` · `sécuriser` · `promouvoir` · `reprendre` · `auditer` · `documenter` · `transférer` · `escalader`

### Words to avoid unless directly evidenced

`révolutionnaire` · `magique` · `autonome` · `garanti` · `sans risque` · `intelligent` used as an empty adjective · `best-in-class` · vague “transformation” without an operational mechanism.

### Tone calibration

- confidence: high
- hype: low
- urgency: low unless a real deadline exists
- specificity: high
- technical density: medium-to-high, with a visible path to plain language
- adversarial posture: low; critique systems, not people
- proof density: high where a claim could affect a decision

## 6. Visual grammar

### OBSERVED homepage tokens

Current homepage CSS uses a dark night-mode system with:

- background: `#07090d`
- panel: `#0d1118`
- elevated panel: `#111823`
- structural line: `#263247`
- primary text: `#f4f7fb`
- muted text: `#a7b1c2`
- cyan accent: `#58d7e7`
- violet accent: `#8b7cff`
- positive/validated accent: `#6de0a1`
- typography: `Inter, system-ui, sans-serif`
- card radius around `18px`; button radius around `12px`
- thin borders, compact tags, evidence bubbles, bounded charts and information-dense cards

### OBSERVED atomic-design motifs

The source-first atomic-design dependency names these signature motifs:

`pulse-line` · `shield-orbit` · `proof-ring` · `data-stream` · `hex-grid` · `orbit-point`

### SYNTHESIS — Visual feeling

**A control room for decisions, not a futuristic poster.**

Use:
- dark surfaces as a neutral inspection environment;
- cyan for evidence/inspection/active system state;
- violet for architecture or system relationship;
- green sparingly for accepted/verified state, never decorative success;
- thin structural lines that expose relationships;
- charts only when they encode bounded, sourced values;
- evidence chips, provenance markers and status labels as recurring interface grammar;
- asymmetry only when it clarifies hierarchy.

Avoid:
- generic AI brains, glowing humanoids, robots, floating holograms;
- abstract “network” visuals with no semantic role;
- fake dashboards with meaningless metrics;
- green “success” everywhere;
- stock-photo trust theater;
- regulatory seals, certification badges or customer logos unless they are actually supported.

## 7. Narrative rhythm

### SYNTHESIS

A strong mickael-umt.com sequence follows this loop:

**Situation → hidden risk → system boundary → mechanism → evidence → residual limit → human owner → next decision.**

This rhythm is more characteristic than any single color or icon. A visual can match the palette and still feel off-brand if it skips boundaries and evidence.

---

## 8. Brand angles

### Angle A — Production is an ownership problem

A demo proves possibility. Production requires a named owner, permissions, observability, evaluation, rollback and transfer.

### Angle B — Evidence is part of the architecture

Proof is not a retrospective report. Provenance, execution evidence and decision traceability are designed into the system.

### Angle C — Reversibility is a trust mechanism

The client should understand how to take over the system, its limits and its recovery path.

### Angle D — Regulated thinking without regulatory theater

The useful inheritance from regulated environments is disciplined evidence, risk-based control and explicit responsibility—not decorative compliance language.

### Angle E — Capability transfer is part of delivery

A mission is incomplete if the client cannot operate, inspect or challenge what was built.

---

## 9. Do / Don't rules

### Do

- Show mechanisms, not just outcomes.
- Expose provenance and proof boundaries.
- Name the human decision point.
- Show before/after as changes in operational state.
- Keep architecture diagrams bounded and readable.
- Use UI-like evidence patterns consistently.
- Make “what this does not prove” visible when necessary.

### Don't

- Add unsupported performance metrics.
- Imply certification or regulatory approval.
- Substitute AI imagery for system explanation.
- Use generic founder hero imagery when a system artifact communicates more.
- Hide uncertainty behind polished language.
- Promise autonomy when the actual value depends on human verification or escalation.

## 10. Decision compass

Every new visual, landing section, ad, deck or product asset should pass this filter:

1. **What decision is this asset helping someone make?**
2. **What system or workflow is being bounded?**
3. **What is inspectable?**
4. **What evidence exists, and what does it not prove?**
5. **Who owns the next action or human decision?**
6. **Can the result be transferred or reversed?**
7. **Does the visual show a real mechanism rather than visualized hype?**

If an asset cannot answer at least the first five, it is probably decorative rather than brand-defining.

---

## 11. Vibe strategy summary

mickael-umt.com should feel like **a technically serious environment where uncertainty gets converted into explicit architecture, evidence and ownership**. The brand earns confidence by showing what can be inspected, how a decision is made, what remains uncertain and how the client takes over.

The emotional identity is therefore not “future-tech excitement.” It is **controlled competence**: the feeling that difficult AI/data work can become understandable, governable, auditable and transferable without pretending uncertainty disappears.

### Portable one-line brief

**Design and write as if the reader will need to operate the system tomorrow and defend the decision six months later.**
