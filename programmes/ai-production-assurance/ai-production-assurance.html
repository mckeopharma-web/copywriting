The moment it becomes a decision problem

Recognise one of these moments — or do not buy the programme yet.

The trigger is not “we want AI.” It is a concrete production decision with a date, an owner and evidence that is currently missing.

01

Demo → production date

A prototype has convinced stakeholders, and someone now has to sign off the real operating conditions.

02

“What evidence supports release?”

Leadership asks what the decision rests on and discovers there is no single evidence package.

03

Security arrived late

Permissions, external tools, memory or data paths are questioned only after the workflow has been built.

04

A pilot incident cannot be reconstructed

The team knows that something went wrong but cannot reliably connect input, model, memory, tools, controls and reviewer decision.

05

Use case #2 is already starting

The organisation is about to replicate a pattern before the first pattern has an explicit control model.

06

The builder is leaving

Operational knowledge, exceptions and launch criteria still live in a person rather than a transferable system.

What fails between demo and operation

A successful prompt is not a production acceptance test.

Agentic and tool-using systems change the assurance problem: prompts, accessible services, tool arguments, permissions, persistent memory and human approvals all become part of the runtime boundary. NCSC’s August 2026 interim guidance is organised around seven operational considerations that include threat modelling, oversight, sandboxing, observability, attribution and emergency shutdown.

Nominal tests miss behavioural variance

In a controlled 2026 laboratory, fixed linguistic reframings produced measured exfiltration attack-success rates spanning 0–100% depending on wording and model. That does not estimate production prevalence; it shows why a release harness must test more than the happy path.

Human approval is a control to evaluate, not a magic word

One controlled user study found that 89.9% of executed overreach in its POLICY condition followed affirmative human approval. The result is study-bounded, but it is enough to reject “human-in-the-loop” as an unevaluated assurance claim.

For whom, under what pressure

A decision unit — not an isolated buyer.

The primary sponsor is typically accountable for moving an AI capability into real operations: CTO, Head of AI, CDO, VP Engineering or transformation lead. The decision is credible only when engineering/data, security or risk, and the business owner can each inspect the boundary that matters to them.

AI / Engineering

“Can this run without heroics?”

Needs explicit architecture, failure modes, evaluation thresholds, observability and rollback.

Security / Risk

“What can it do, reach and disclose?”

NCSC guidance explicitly calls out the prompt plus reachable networks and services as threat-modelling inputs before deployment.

Business owner

“What decision am I signing?”

Needs the target outcome, acceptance threshold, residual risk, owner and a record of why the chosen release state is justified.

If your organisation is subject to the EU AI Act, legal obligations depend on the system’s classification and transition rules. The Commission’s current overview distinguishes four risk levels and identifies logging, human oversight, robustness and cybersecurity among high-risk requirements. This programme is engineering assurance, not legal advice.
Before you reserve anything

The mandate only works when the evidence can actually be reached.

Good fit

  • A real decision is in play: release, limited release, renewal, audit preparation, replacement or controlled expansion.
  • An executive sponsor can resolve scope conflicts and accept the work product.
  • System, data, logs and documentation can be accessed under appropriate confidentiality controls.
  • At least one internal technical owner can work through the handover.

Wrong fit

  • Trend briefing, inspiration talk or generic prompting course.
  • Enterprise-wide “AI transformation” without one bounded use case.
  • A statutory approval or certification that only an authorised role can issue.
  • No access to the evidence needed to evaluate the system.

A no-fit decision in qualification is cheaper than discovering the boundary after implementation has started.

The logic before the tools

Build the decision system before asking the committee for a decision.

AI Production Assurance is a 12-week architecture, implementation and transfer mandate for one critical use case. The mechanism is the Production Readiness Evidence Loop™: a reusable operating loop that binds acceptance criteria, controls, evaluation, runtime evidence and human accountability to the same release decision.

1 · FrameDecision contractUse case, baseline, owner, failure conditions, acceptance criteria.
2 · DesignBoundariesData, models, tools, memory, identities, permissions, dependencies.
3 · ControlPromotion gatesPolicy, tests, exceptions, review, rollback, evidence capture.
4 · TestContext-specific evaluationFunctional, regression, adversarial and operational scenarios.
5 · TransferInternal operationRunbooks, owners, handover exercise, next-use-case backlog.

NIST’s AI RMF deliberately separates risk work into four non-sequential functions — Govern, Map, Measure and Manage — rather than treating governance as a final checklist. NIST’s 2026 TEVV-Athlon draft likewise frames context-specific AI assessment as a four-stage method and explicitly includes agentic systems in scope. The programme translates that operating logic into the concrete production boundary of one use case.

Five work products — each bounded by an acceptance criterion.

03

Governance layer

Named owners, scoped capabilities, credential/permission lifecycle, human decision boundaries and rollback conditions.

04

Execution evidence

Within the instrumented scope: versions, sources, tool calls, decisions, exceptions and reviewer sign-off captured so a release decision can be reconstructed.

Handover package

Runbooks, operating responsibilities, known limitations, backlog and one supervised pass of the loop by the internal team.

Why data minimisation belongs in the deliverable

A 2026 study of 25 unannotated MCP schemas reported a 79.0% reduction in its own privacy-cost metric after schema-aware argument minimisation. The evidence supports testing the mechanism; it does not justify a generic “79% safer” claim.

NCSC guidance also treats runtime telemetry as an assurance input: agent traces/transcripts plus wider sandbox logs for active oversight and later investigation, with logs protected and immutable where possible.

Before / after

The programme changes decision state, not marketing language.

Before

  • Prototype ownership is informal.
  • Permissions and data reach are only partly known.
  • Acceptance criteria are negotiated during release.
  • Regressions are found by users or ad-hoc tests.
  • Knowledge of failures and exceptions lives in people.
→

After the mandate

  • Decision owner and escalation path are named.
  • Capabilities, identities and external reach are documented.
  • Promotion thresholds and exception rules are versioned.
  • Evaluation runs before promotion and leaves evidence.
  • Runbook, known limitations and handover state are explicit.

  • Architecture, data/tool boundaries, identity and permissions.
  • Evaluation harness, observability, execution evidence and rollback conditions.
  • Promotion evidence package and internal handover.

Explicitly out of scope

Why authorization is outside the model

In the cited compromised-model evaluation, observed AgentDojo exfiltration fell from 75–100% to 0% across the reported domains when authorization was enforced outside the model, and the same study reports 544/544 evaluated InjecAgent data-stealing cases blocked.

The design implication is bounded: consequential actions should have enforceable authorization beyond model intent. It does not mean this architecture eliminates all production attacks.

What can be guaranteed honestly

Guarantee the work product — never the decision of an authority or the behaviour of a foundation model.

Written scope before billing

Boundary, exclusions, dependencies and acceptance criteria are agreed before work begins.

Acceptance-based rework

A deliverable that does not meet its agreed acceptance criterion is corrected; the criterion is not rewritten after delivery.

Reversibility

Source, configuration, schemas, decision records and runbooks remain in client-controlled repositories unless the contract explicitly says otherwise.

Human decision boundary

Every consequential workflow states where automation ends and accountable human authority begins. That boundary is tested rather than assumed.

Tool-neutral core

Acceptance criteria and evidence identity are expressed independently of one model/vendor so a tool change does not erase the decision model.

Explicit non-guarantees

Questions to resolve before engagement

Objections are design inputs, not copywriting problems.

Why not give this to our internal data team?

You may. The programme is useful when the missing work is not model building but the cross-functional assurance layer: bounded authority, evaluation, release criteria, evidence capture, rollback and decision ownership. Existing modules you already control are explicitly removed from scope.

Will governance slow experimentation?

The programme separates upstream experimentation from promotion. The purpose of gates is to make the release boundary explicit; no claim is made that governance automatically reduces cycle time. NCSC guidance places threat modelling, oversight, sandboxing, observability and response planning in the operating design rather than after an incident.

We already use ChatGPT or another model. Is that enough?

A model interface is not an operating control system. Production assurance concerns the system around it: data paths, tool authority, memory, secrets, external services, evaluation, logs, human decisions and rollback.

How do we measure ROI?

We first establish the baseline and define measurements that your organisation can actually observe — for example cycle time, manual review effort, error/rework, service quality, cost and operational risk indicators. We do not promise a percentage improvement before a comparable baseline exists.

Do you guarantee regulatory compliance?

No. The programme can engineer controls, records and technical evidence that support a compliance process. Legal classification, certification and statutory approvals remain with the competent roles and authorities.

How many use cases are covered?

One priority use case in the core mandate. A second case is a replication decision after the first loop has generated a stable pattern.

Can we work under NDA?

Yes. Public capability proof is separated from client architecture, datasets, findings and production metrics.

What remains after you leave?

The agreed architecture and control definitions, evaluation assets, evidence schema, runbooks, known limitations, decision record, backlog and named internal owners.
