What it costs, and why the workaround does not hold

What it costs, and why the obvious workaround does not hold.

Every blocker is answered further down — in the offers, the sequence or the commitments. None is left open.

Compliance arrives after the product

Writing documentation afterwards produces a document describing what was done, not what should have been. The gap is discovered at the client’s, at the worst moment.

The usual workaround, and its limit

Hiring a quality profile at the end of the chain moves the problem: they document decisions they did not take part in.

The client need is translated twice

The pharma domain speaks risk, the product speaks feature, and nobody speaks both. Each translation loses the constraint that mattered.

The usual workaround, and its limit

Adding a validation committee adds delay without adding the competence missing at the interface.

Evidence is not a sprint deliverable

The feature is “done” when it works, not when it is defensible. Evidence debt accumulates sprint after sprint and is paid at audit.

The usual workaround, and its limit

Adding a “documentation” column to the board changes nothing while the definition of done excludes it.

This is the right mandate when

  • ✓ A real decision is at stake: a promotion to production, an audit, a renewal, a launch or a system replacement.
  • ✓ A named sponsor can arbitrate scope and sign off on acceptance.
  • ✓ Access to the necessary systems, data or documents is possible under NDA within the first three weeks.
  • ✓ You build a product sold to pharma clients, and their regulatory requirements land in your backlog.

This is the wrong mandate when

  • ✕ You want a trend overview or market watch: the short decision-clinic format costs less and answers better.
  • ✕ The expected outcome depends on a regulatory signature only a statutory role inside your organisation can hold.
  • ✕ No access to evidence is possible: with no data, no logs and no documents there is nothing to make defensible.
  • ✕ You want extra development capacity: this mandate works on product decisions and evidence, not on velocity.

A “no” said at this stage costs twenty minutes. Said after the quote, it costs everyone a quarter.

The logic, before the tools

Turn pharma-client decisions into product features whose evidence, permissions, validation and ownership are designed with the product.

A backlog fed by feedback loses track of the business risk that justified the feature. I help Product Managers, CPOs and HealthTech product leadership restore that trace by connecting pharma feedback and product backlog to user stories, permissions, acceptance criteria, human validation and defendable sprint evidence. The Decision-to-Feature Trace™ (Decision → Requirement → Feature → Control → Proof) structures each user story: business risk, authoritative source, user story, access control, acceptance criteria, human validation and sprint evidence stay connected end to end. The product remains HealthTech & PharmaTech Product Engineering; its role is to build the bridge between Product, Quality, Data and AI Governance, not merely to translate feedback.

Why this model exists — but only where the scope applies

The offer does not treat “pharma” as an automatic regulatory classification. The applicable regime is qualified first; only then are its risk, traceability, testing and documentation requirements translated into product work.

What the intersection removes

The differentiator is the intersection, not the addition.

What the combination buys is not “more skills”: it is one less translation between the problem and the evidence.

  • Seam removed The pharmaceutical risk → product requirement translation happens without an intermediary, by someone who has worked both sides.
  • Seam removed Evidence is specified with the feature, therefore estimated with it, therefore actually delivered.
  • Seam removed AI controls are set by someone who builds those systems, not inferred from a general principle.

These are mechanisms, not measured effects: removing a translation removes an occasion to lose the constraint. The gain itself depends on your organisation.

Evaluate the engagement

What is guaranteed, and what cannot be

What is guaranteed is the work product, never an authority’s outcome.

Scope written before invoicing

Scope, exclusions, dependencies and acceptance criteria are written and agreed before the first billed day. A scope change is an amendment, never an end-of-engagement surprise.

Deliverables complete or reworked

A deliverable that fails its acceptance criteria is reworked until it passes, at no additional charge. The criteria are written before production, not after.

Reversibility on exit

Code, data, schemas, decisions and logs are yours and leave with you. No proprietary component, no licence, no hosting the continuation of the work would depend on.

Confidentiality boundary

Any deliverable or exercise handling your data carries a classification and an explicit rule: permitted, anonymised, synthetic or forbidden. The rule is written before the handling, not observed after it.

Human-decision boundary

Every case handled states where automated assistance stops and where human validation is mandatory. A system that decides without that boundary written down is not delivered.

Tool-neutral core

Objectives, acceptance criteria and evidence are written independently of any given vendor or model. A tool change is an amendment, never a restart of the investment.

What is not guaranteed, and cannot honestly be

  • Acceptance by your end client is not guaranteed: it depends on their own quality system.
  • Team velocity is not guaranteed; the format adds work at refinement and removes it at audit.

What is left to settle

What is left to settle.

The questions that change scope, price or accountability are already answered above. Here, the rest.

01 Won’t the format slow the sprints down?

It adds time at refinement and removes it at rework and audit. Phase 3 measures both on two or three real features before generalising.

02 Do you build the features?

No, that is explicitly out of scope. The mandate covers the product decision, the specification and the evidence; implementation stays with your team.

03 Our clients have contradictory requirements. How do we arbitrate?

By tracing them back to the underlying product risk, where they almost always converge. What genuinely stays divergent becomes a documented option, not a code branch.

04 Do you engage with our clients?

On request and under NDA, supporting your team. The client relationship stays yours, and so does any commitment made in front of them.
