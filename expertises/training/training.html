02 · Consequences

The failure mode is an unmeasured operating gap.

70.89% of EU non-users that had considered AI cited lack of relevant expertise; legal and privacy concerns were also common. This does not prove training alone fixes adoption.

A quiz verifies recall; a charter documents rules. Neither alone proves a real task is executed under data, approval and escalation constraints.

Working synthesis, not a verbatim: “I need to show what changed in the work, not how many people attended.”

Frustrations & fears

Training activity is visible but task competence is not; AI use spreads before permissions, confidentiality boundaries and escalation are operational; a pilot may scale before evidence exists; the programme can remain trainer-dependent.

Wants & aspirations

Observable competence on named tasks; role-specific boundaries for automate / verify / escalate; evidence supporting extend / correct / stop decisions; reusable assets that internal teams can own.

Key purchase drivers

Must-have: task baseline, situated practice, pre-written rubric, controlled evaluation, workplace transfer observation, data classification, human/AI matrix and handover. Deal-killers: generic awareness, no real task, no sponsor, no evidence access, no observation window, or consultant-held Qualiopi as a hard requirement.

Decision unit

Decision-maker: transformation/L&D sponsor when budget and mandate sit there. Advocate: AI-adoption or functional lead. Champion: manager or practitioner who needs the workflow to work. Gatekeepers: security, privacy, compliance and procurement. Final budget authority remains organisation-specific and must be validated.

12 · Process

Five phases, five decisions.

  1. Baseline — measure the named task.
  2. Design — exercises, rubrics, data classification, thresholds.
  3. Pilot — ≤12, then co-facilitation.
  4. Controlled evaluation — outside practice context.
  5. Transfer review — observe work and decide extend/correct/stop.
