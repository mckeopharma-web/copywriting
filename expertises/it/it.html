Turn a working prototype into a production state your team can operate, challenge and defend.

For CTO, AI Platform, Data and Security leaders approaching a production decision: define the operating context first, then make evaluation, authority, data provenance and release evidence explicit. NIST’s August 2026 TEVV-Athlon draft uses a four-stage method for context-specific AI assessment and explicitly includes agentic systems in scope.

Decision rule. Start with the production property that must hold. Use the least-complex mechanism that can enforce, observe or prove it. A conventional database, ordinary access-control layer or standard release gate remains the right answer when it is sufficient.

Customer avatar · primary

Production-bound IT decision lead.

Role-first profile: CTO / Head of AI Platform / Head of Data / AI Security or trust decision-maker carrying a functioning AI or data prototype toward production, release, governance or external verification.

“I need to know what can go to production, under which controls, and how I will defend that decision.”Synthesized decision-language — not a customer verbatim.

Frustrations & fears

  • Agents work in demos, but ownership, permissions, memory and evaluation are not yet controllable.
  • Teams produce conflicting answers because lineage, definitions or versions are dispersed.
  • Security arrives as a late report instead of a release condition.
  • A reviewer or partner may need proof without receiving sensitive internals.

Wants & aspirations

  • Observable, bounded runtime with explicit human authority.
  • Traceable data and replayable decision evidence.
  • Promotion gates with rollback criteria.
  • Independent verification only where the trust boundary requires it.

Key purchase drivers

  • Fit the existing stack before adding infrastructure.
  • Explicit acceptance criteria, evidence and ownership.
  • Maintainability and knowledge transfer to the internal team.
  • Bounded scope and transparent estimation before commitment.

Deal-killers

  • Technology-first recommendations with no buyer decision attached.
  • Opaque data flows, hidden authority or security added after implementation.
  • Unverifiable ROI or “secure by default” claims.
  • Replacing a working platform when a narrower control would solve the problem.

Decision unit

Recurring collaborators modeled across the specialist avatars: AI Infrastructure, AI Reliability/SRE, Runtime Engineering, CISO/Security, Data Platform, Product, FinOps/Capacity and Research/Model Platform. The primary avatar may be the decision-maker, advocate or technical champion depending on the organization.

Blockchain & Verifiable Computing

Use signatures, attestations, shared state, smart contracts or zero-knowledge mechanisms only when another party must establish a bounded property independently. If the same acceptance property can be met by conventional infrastructure, keep the simpler system.

The commercial question is not “which blockchain?” It is “what must another party verify, what may remain private, and what is the minimum mechanism that satisfies that trust boundary?”
