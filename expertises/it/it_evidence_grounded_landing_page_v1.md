# IT & Data Platforms — Evidence-Grounded Landing Page Upgrade

## Positioning invariant

This category page is a routing and production-readiness page, not generic IT consulting and not a replacement for the four underlying specialist pages. Preserve four lanes: Agentic AI Engineering; Data Engineering & Evidence Architecture; AI Security, Assurance & DevSecOps; Blockchain & Verifiable Computing.

Category-level promise: move a critical AI/data system from a working prototype to a state that can be operated, governed, reviewed and handed over with explicit evidence. Do not imply that every problem requires AI, blockchain, ZK or a new data platform. Mechanism selection remains conditional on the decision boundary.

## Elevator pitch

We engineer the production boundary around critical AI and data systems for CTO, AI Platform, Data and Security leaders whose prototypes work but are not yet controllable enough to operate with confidence. The engagement connects runtime observability, explicit authority, traceable data, evaluation and security gates and—only when justified—independent verification, so the team can make and defend a production decision with evidence rather than assumptions.

# C1–C19

## [C1] Offer

### IT Production Boundary Engagement

Take one critical AI/data system from “it works” to “we can operate and defend this state.” Begin with the production decision, not a predetermined technology. Identify the boundary, map authority and data flows, define acceptance evidence, implement the controls that matter and leave an operating model that does not depend on hidden consultant knowledge.

Typical scope can combine agentic runtime architecture and permissions; data lineage, quality and evidence stores; evaluation harnesses and promotion thresholds; CI/CD, SBOM, infrastructure, security and rollback gates; signatures, attestations, smart contracts or ZK only when independent verification is justified. Default to the minimum sufficient mechanism.

## [C2] Who it is for

Primary buyer: CTO / Head of AI Platform / Head of Data / AI Security or trust decision-maker.

Fit condition: a functioning prototype is approaching production, release, governance review or external verification and the buyer is accountable for the state that follows.

Agentic route: permissions, memory, orchestration, review or execution behavior are not yet controllable. Data route: sources and transformations cannot be reconstructed reliably. Security route: release is approaching while evidence arrives too late. Verifiable route: another party must establish a bounded property without simply trusting the operator or receiving all sensitive data.

## [C3] Product

This is a category-level engagement combining four specialist capabilities as required.

**Agentic AI Engineering:** tool routing, MCP boundaries, governed memory, human review, evaluation, execution traces and service operations.

**Data Engineering & Evidence Architecture:** schemas, ETL/ELT, events, quality controls, lineage, evidence stores and auditable APIs.

**AI Security, Assurance & DevSecOps:** threat models, dependency/container checks, SBOM, policy gates, evaluation criteria, secrets, infrastructure controls and rollback.

**Blockchain & Verifiable Computing:** signatures, attestations, smart contracts, shared state, selective disclosure or ZK only when an independent verifier needs them. A conventional database remains correct when shared verification is unnecessary.

## [C4] Why the buyer needs it

The buyer wants a production decision with visible assumptions; explicit authority; reconstructible data and transformations; testable release/rollback criteria; operational visibility; transferable handover; independent verification where required; and architecture trade-offs that can be explained to technical and executive stakeholders.

## [C5] Delivery method

1. **Decision Boundary & Acceptance Contract** — decision, scope, non-goals, properties and acceptance evidence.
2. **Runtime, Identity & Authority Model** — services, agents, tools, identities, permissions, delegation and human intervention.
3. **Data Lineage & Evidence Architecture** — sources, schemas, transformations, quality rules, events and provenance.
4. **Evaluation & Regression Harness** — representative cases, adversarial scenarios, grading and promotion thresholds.
5. **Security / DevSecOps Promotion Gates** — threat boundaries, secrets, supply-chain checks, infrastructure checks and rollback conditions.
6. **Verification Mechanism Decision** — database, signature, attestation, shared ledger, selective disclosure or ZK according to the trust boundary.
7. **Observability, Incident & Rollback Model** — execution, policy decisions, errors, review queues and operating evidence.
8. **Acceptance Evidence & Capability Transfer** — acceptance run, residual risks, runbooks, evidence paths, decision rights and ownership.

## [C6] Benefits

Fewer hidden production assumptions; explicit authority boundaries; reconstructible data and decisions; measurable release criteria; security evidence on the delivery path; observability tied to operations; documented reasons for choosing or rejecting distributed verification; reusable tests, runbooks, evidence and ownership. These are engineering outputs and control properties, not guaranteed business outcomes.

## [C7] Eight modules

Decision Boundary & Acceptance Contract; Runtime, Identity & Authority Model; Data Lineage & Evidence Architecture; Evaluation & Regression Harness; Security/DevSecOps Promotion Gates; Verification Mechanism Decision; Observability/Incident/Rollback Model; Acceptance Evidence & Capability Transfer.

## [C8] Three outcomes

1. **Defensible production decision:** state what is ready, bounded, uncertain and evidenced.
2. **Controllable production state:** make authority, data flow, release conditions and runtime behavior explicit enough to operate and review.
3. **Transferable technical assets:** leave tests, runbooks, evidence paths, architecture decisions and ownership with the operating team.

## [C9] Problems → positioning

1. Production acceptance is implicit → write the decision and acceptance contract first.
2. Agent permissions exceed task needs → externalize authority and delegation constraints.
3. Data answers differ → build lineage, contracts, quality controls and evidence records.
4. Evaluation is inconsistent → executable regression and adversarial evaluation contract.
5. Security arrives after the release candidate → promotion gates with inspectable artifacts.
6. Logs cannot explain why an action was allowed → bind runtime events to identity, policy, state and tools.
7. DLT is proposed before the trust boundary → define verifier/property/boundary before mechanism.
8. Operational ownership is vague → bind components to decision rights, escalation and rollback.
9. Handover is documentation-only → transfer tests, runbooks, evidence paths and ownership with code.
10. Mixed problem, unclear offer → use this category as the router into the narrowest specialist lane.

## [C10] Direct solutions

Write acceptance before expanding controls; define authority as a system property rather than a prompt instruction; make provenance/version identity first-class; convert demonstrations into regression gates; put security evidence on the promotion path; make consequential actions reconstructible; require verifier/property/trust-boundary analysis before blockchain/ZK; assign operating and rollback authority; treat handover artifacts as production assets; route to the narrowest matching specialist lane.

## [C11] Scarcity

Availability is confirmed during qualification because embedded engineering capacity is finite by construction.

## [C16] FAQ

**Do we need to choose a lane before the first call?** No. Start with the production constraint; the specialist lane follows.

**Can you work with an existing stack?** Yes. Replacement is not the default.

**Do you always recommend blockchain for verifiability?** No. If a database, signature, attestation or conventional controlled system satisfies the trust boundary, distributed consensus is unnecessary complexity.

**Can you guarantee an AI system will be secure?** No. Threat boundaries, controls, evaluations and evidence can be engineered; absence of all future vulnerabilities cannot be guaranteed.

**What does observable mean?** Relevant execution state, policy decisions, tool actions, errors and review points are captured in a form operators can use.

**What is governed memory?** Scoped state with ownership, retention, access and review rules rather than an unbounded context store.

**How is lineage used?** To reconstruct source, transformation, version and consuming decision.

**What is a promotion gate?** A release condition that must pass before a build, model, agent configuration or infrastructure change advances.

**How is ZK justified?** By the property a verifier must establish, allowed disclosure, trust assumptions and whether simpler mechanisms already satisfy the requirement.

**What remains after handover?** The scoped architecture decisions, tests/evaluations, runbooks, policy/release criteria, evidence paths and ownership documentation.
