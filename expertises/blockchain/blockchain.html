Triggers

When “put it on-chain” is not yet a requirement.

The useful trigger is a verification problem, not a technology preference. Start when one of these situations becomes material.

01

A counterparty must verify a claim

They need independent acceptance, but handing over the full source dataset is not acceptable.

02

Your own registry is the trust bottleneck

The audit trail exists, but the party evaluating it still has to trust the operator who controls it.

03

Authority can outlive the design assumptions

Keys, delegates, smart accounts and admin rights need explicit recovery, revocation and separation-of-duty rules.

04

The board asks the right question

“What could another party establish if we were unavailable, compromised or simply disputed the history?”

Consequences

Judge programmability by delivery properties, not by blockchain volume.

The mechanism only earns its complexity when it changes a bounded operational property: settlement, coordination, disclosure, authorization or independent verification.

Qualification

Good fit when another party must not simply trust your operator.

Strong fit

  • Multiple organizations need a shared or independently verifiable state.
  • A property must be verified while the underlying data stays confidential.
  • Programmable settlement or lifecycle coordination has a bounded operational purpose.
  • Key authority, delegation, recovery or revocation materially changes risk.
  • A third party needs an acceptance test it can reproduce.

Probably not blockchain

  • One accountable operator controls all writes and all readers already trust it.
  • The primary requirement is generic CRUD, search or high-throughput internal storage.
  • No independent verifier, counterparty or shared-state boundary exists.
  • The business case depends mainly on token price, speculative issuance or vague “transparency”.
  • A signed record plus an auditable conventional database satisfies the acceptance test.
Proposition

From claim to proof, with the minimum sufficient mechanism.

The engagement converts an ambiguous trust claim into an independently testable property, then selects the least complex mechanism that can satisfy it.

Questions

The four questions that usually decide the architecture.

Do we need to publish the underlying data?

No. The architecture can keep source data off-chain and expose only the commitment, attestation or proof required by the verifier. Whether ZK is justified depends on the exact disclosure constraint.

Does privacy automatically mean zero-knowledge?

No. Signatures, selective disclosure, access control, commitments or conventional confidential infrastructure can be sufficient. ZK adds proving-system complexity and should solve a specific verifier/privacy problem.

What if the conclusion is “do not use blockchain”?

That is a valid paid outcome. The engagement is an architecture decision and delivery service, not a quota for distributed ledgers.

Who audits the final system?

Independent formal audit is a separate role unless explicitly contracted. This engagement can prepare the architecture, tests, evidence package and verifier interface for that audit.
