When this becomes a problem

You recognise one of these operating moments.

A safety source can be authoritative and still arrive on a cadence that is not your review cadence. openFDA documents quarterly API updates and warns of a lag of three months or more.

01

The signal appears elsewhere first

A regulator, literature source or external dataset creates a candidate before your internal path surfaces it.

02

E2B(R3) work is re-entered

Stable source fields still trigger manual extraction, normalization or transfer work.

03

The escalation threshold is implicit

The rule, version and rationale are not reconstructible.

04

The dashboard is not the evidence

Source → transform → threshold → reviewer → decision cannot be replayed.

Consequences

The failure mode is broken continuity between data and judgment.

GVP Module IX requires signal-management audit trails to preserve dates/timeliness, analyses, decisions and rationale.

Review capacity is consumed upstream

Collection, duplicate handling, coding and re-entry compete with expert review.

Thresholds become institutional memory

Comparable observations can be treated differently without an explicit versioned rule.

A score becomes implicit authority

The risk is allowing a ranking or alert to silently become the decision.

For whom

The accountable operator holding signal, evidence and decision together.

For a pharmacovigilance lead, the target is controlled throughput with explicit performance governance—not maximum automation. GVP calls for performance control and, when used, performance indicators in the PSMF annex.

Scope

Automation stops before medical signal judgment.

EMA states that a safety signal does not itself mean the medicine caused the reported adverse event.

In scope

Source ingestion · normalization · NLP extraction · prioritisation · threshold governance · exception queues · lineage · decision log · handover.

Out of scope

Medical causality assessment · autonomous signal validation · QPPV responsibility · PSUR/PBRER authorship as substitute service · exhaustive-detection guarantees.

Engagement boundaries

What production-ready means here.

Acceptance is attached to scope, traceability, reversibility, confidentiality and a human-decision boundary.

Confidentiality and integrity

GVP requires confidentiality, security, validity and data integrity, including during inter-organisation transfer.

Data minimisation

A connector should not ingest personal data merely because it can reach it; GDPR Article 5 requires data to be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary.

Vendor-neutral kernel

Source contracts, decision lineage and review semantics should survive a model, vendor or orchestration change.

No exhaustiveness guarantee

No detector is marketed here as proving complete signal capture; sensitivity remains bound to evaluated data and conditions.

Questions

What the system does—and does not decide.

AI in pharmacovigilance is an explicit topic in the HMA–EMA 2026–2028 data workplan, but a workplan is planned regulatory activity, not proof that any implementation is compliant.

Can the model confirm a signal?

No. It can assist extraction, ranking and evidence assembly; medical and regulatory judgment remains human-owned.

Can data stay in the client environment?

Yes, if source systems and target architecture permit it; minimise movement and disclosure.

How are thresholds justified?

Definition, version, owner, rationale, calibration evidence where available, and an exception route.

Do we need to replace the safety database?

Not by default. The engagement can wrap existing safety systems with governed ingestion, transformation and review services.
