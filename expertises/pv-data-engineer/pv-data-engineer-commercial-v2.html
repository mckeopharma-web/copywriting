Scope

The automation boundary is part of the product.

EMA states that a safety signal does not itself mean the medicine caused the reported adverse event. The engagement therefore stops before autonomous medical causality or signal confirmation.

In scope

Source ingestion · normalisation · NLP extraction · prioritisation · threshold governance · exception queues · lineage · decision log · handover.

Out of scope

Medical causality assessment · autonomous signal validation · QPPV responsibility · PSUR/PBRER authorship as a substitute service · exhaustive-detection guarantees.

FAQ

Questions that change the responsibility boundary.

The answers below are intentionally direct because ambiguity here becomes implementation risk later.

Can the model confirm a signal?

No. It can assist extraction, ranking, evidence assembly and exception routing; medical and regulatory judgment remains human-owned.

Can safety data stay in our environment?

Yes when the source systems and target architecture permit it. The design preference is to minimise movement and disclosure rather than centralise data by default.

How are thresholds justified?

By definition, version, owner, rationale, calibration evidence where available, and an explicit exception route.

Do we need to replace the safety database?

Not by default. The engagement can wrap the existing safety system with governed ingestion, transformation, prioritisation and review services.

Current regulatory context for AI in pharmacovigilance

AI in pharmacovigilance is an explicit topic in the HMA–EMA 2026–2028 data workplan, but a workplan is planned regulatory activity, not proof that any implementation is compliant.
