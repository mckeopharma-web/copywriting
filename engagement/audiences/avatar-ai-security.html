Decision avatar 02 · AI governance / risk / security

From shadow AI to release evidence you can defend.

Marc Lefèvre owns the gap between AI systems that can act and an organisation that still cannot state—quickly and reproducibly—who authorised what, under which controls, with which evidence.

The source audience page defines this role as “Responsable gouvernance, risque ou sécurité IA” and frames the transition from opaque automation, shadow AI and excessive permissions to explicit ownership, bounded systems and versioned release evidence.

Triggers

Security becomes a decision problem when the system can act before the control can answer.

The mandate opens around a concrete release, launch, audit, renewal or production boundary—not around generic concern.

01

Release ownership is unclear

Nobody can state whether the AI component, its tools and permissions were reviewed.

02

An agent gains a connector

An MCP connector expands reachable data, credentials or side effects without entering the release boundary.

03

A customer asks for evidence

Dependencies, versions and provenance have to be rebuilt from logs and conversations.

04

Security arrives after the choice

Remediation competes directly with release timing.

The UK NCSC stated on 4 August 2026 that AI systems need strong safeguards, real-time oversight and clear response plans from the outset, and that detection alone after an incident is insufficient.

Consequences

The missing object is not another report. It is an enforceable decision boundary.

Prompting is not the control plane

Consequential actions also require technical and operational enforcement outside the model.

The attack surface is wider than code

Tools, identities, network reach, memory, connectors and accessible data can change exposure without a source-code change.

Human approval is not automatically safe

The approval point, context and action consequence must themselves be evaluated.

NCSC’s August 2026 guidance says threat modelling should happen before deployment and explicitly includes the prompt plus the networks and services the agent can access.

Decision role

Marc Lefèvre is a decision structure, not a demographic persona.

Source avatar

AI Governance / Risk / Security Lead

The source page says these avatars structure message and offer; they require validation by interviews, CRM and observed behaviour.

Decision pressure

Move from opaque automation to bounded, attributable release decisions.

He needs to know who owns the system, which actions are permitted, what can be reached, what blocks a release and what evidence remains after promotion.

Qualification

Conditions before a quote.

Good fit

✓ Real release, audit, renewal or launch decision.
✓ Named sponsor can arbitrate scope and residual risk.
✓ Evidence and systems accessible under NDA.
✓ Delivery chain can enforce blocking conditions.

Not this mandate

✕ One-off penetration test or incident-response retainer.
✕ Statutory signature only the client can provide.
✕ No access to the evidence boundary.
✕ General awareness without a decision event.

Process

Four phases. Four stopping points with a usable artifact.

Surface

Inventory agents, connectors, credentials and reachable data. Output: boundary + threat model.

Gate

Translate relevant controls into blocking conditions. Output: live release gate.

Evidence

Bind promotion to policy, results, exceptions, artifact identity and approver.

Handoff

The team adds a control and executes an exception path without the consultant.

Control blocks

Select modules from the risk boundary—not from a fixed checklist.

Threat modelling

Objective, action surface, reachable services, prompts/context, failure scenarios and red lines.

Agent isolation & authority

Capability boundaries, network restrictions, credential scope, escalation and external authorization where justified.

Supply chain & promotion

SBOM, dependencies, images, secrets, policy predicates, exceptions and rollback criteria.

Observability & release evidence

Runtime traces, sandbox logs, artifact identity, decision journals and accountable approval.

In the Bounded Agents preprint’s compromised-model AgentDojo evaluation, external authorization reduced observed exfiltration attack-success rate to 0% across every reported exfiltration category.

NCSC’s August 2026 guidance names agent traces or transcripts and wider sandbox logs as telemetry for active oversight and later investigation; it says logs should be protected and immutable where possible.

Intersection

The differentiator is the seam removed between policy, engineering and evidence.

Control ↔ pipeline

The policy is expressed so the delivery chain can actually block or allow a release.

Authority ↔ runtime

Permissions, tools and reachable systems are engineered, not questionnaire-only abstractions.

Requirement ↔ evidence

The evidence artifact is designed with the control, not assembled afterwards.

Questions

What still changes scope, responsibility or adoption.

Will a blocking gate stop the team?

It can; that is why the gate maps to a consequential risk and ships with a traceable exception path.

Must we replace Jenkins, GitLab or GitHub Actions?

No. The policy is designed around the delivery chain in place unless migration is itself the decision.

Is human approval enough?

No single control is assumed sufficient. Approval context, authority and runtime enforcement must be designed together.

Can we start with a short diagnostic?

Yes. A bounded system/authority inventory and threat model can be the first stopping point.
